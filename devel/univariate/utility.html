    Residual Diagnostics¶

    If a model is correctly specified, the standardized residuals \(\hat{e}_t = \hat{\epsilon}_t / \hat{\sigma}_t\) behave like draws from the distribution of the model, and so the tails of the residuals can be compared to the tails of the distribution that was assumed. The functions below summarize the tails of standardized residuals, and are also available for the results of a model, which use the standardized residuals and the estimated distribution of the model.

    • excess_kurtosis() is the sample excess kurtosis, the fourth central moment divided by the squared variance, less 3. It is 0 for a Normal, and is positive when the residuals have heavier tails than a Normal. Observations that are not finite are ignored.

    • hill_estimator() is the Hill estimator of the tail index, using the k largest absolute residuals, and int(sqrt(n)) of them if k is not provided. The estimator is \(\hat{\nu} = \left(k^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ln X_{(n-i+1)} - \ln X_{(n-k)}\right)^{-1}\), where \(X_{(j)}\) is the \(j\)-th smallest absolute residual. Smaller values indicate heavier tails. The tail index of a Student’s t distribution is its degrees of freedom.

    • var_ratio() is the ratio of the Value-at-Risk (VaR) of a Normal to the VaR of the distribution of the model. It is the 99.9% VaR unless p, the probability of an exception, is provided. A ratio below 1 means that the Normal understates the VaR of the model’s distribution. It depends on the distribution and its parameters only, and not on residuals.

    The results of a model have the same diagnostics. They use the standardized residuals, std_resid, and, for var_ratio, the estimated distribution and parameters.

    Backtesting Value-at-Risk¶

    The functions below compare a series of Value-at-Risk (VaR) forecasts with the returns that were realized. VaR is reported as a positive loss (a 99% VaR of 2.5 is the loss that is exceeded with probability 1%), and an observation is an exception if the loss exceeds the VaR, \(-r_t > \mathrm{VaR}_t\). VaR is named by its confidence level, and p is always the probability of an exception, the size of the tail: p=0.05 for a 95% VaR and p=0.01 for a 99% VaR. It must be in (0, 0.5], so that a confidence level such as 0.99 raises an error. p is 0.05 by default, except in the Basel traffic light, whose default is the 99% VaR of the framework. A quantile of the returns, which is negative in the lower tail, must be negated first. The returns and the VaR must be finite.

    If both are Series they are aligned on the index, and the observations that they have in common are used. Otherwise they must have the same length and are matched by position. The forecasts of forecast() are indexed by the date of the forecast origin by default, so the one-step forecast for day \(t\) has the label of day \(t-1\). Use forecast(..., align="target"), or shift the VaR forward by one observation, so that each VaR has the label of the return that it forecasts.

    • var_exceptions() is the bool array of exceptions, or a Series if the inputs are pandas objects.

    • kupiec_pof() is the proportion-of-failures likelihood-ratio test of Kupiec (1995) that the probability of an exception is p. It is \(\chi^2_1\), and it is two-sided, so that too few exceptions also reject.

    • christoffersen_independence() is the likelihood-ratio test of Christoffersen (1998) that the exceptions are independent, against a first-order Markov chain. It is \(\chi^2_1\).

    • christoffersen_conditional_coverage() is the sum of the two statistics, which tests the coverage and the independence jointly. It is \(\chi^2_2\).

    • traffic_light() is the zone of the framework of the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (1996) for the exceptions in the last window observations (250 by default). The zone is green if the cumulative binomial probability of the number of exceptions is below 95%, yellow if it is at least 95% and below 99.99%, and red otherwise. For 250 observations and p=0.01 green is 0 to 4 exceptions, yellow is 5 to 9 and red is 10 or more, as in Table 2 of the framework. It returns a TrafficLightResult with the zone, the number of exceptions, the cumulative probability and the probability of a type 1 error, which is the probability of that number of exceptions or more. A series that is shorter than window raises an error.

    The three tests return a WaldTestStatistic. The likelihood-ratio statistics are computed with \(0\ln 0=0\), so that a series with no exception, or with only exceptions, has a statistic.
