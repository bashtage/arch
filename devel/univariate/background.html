    Theoretical Background¶

    The models in arch.univariate divide an observed series \(r_t\), usually a return, into a mean, a conditional volatility and a standardized shock

    \begin{eqnarray} r_t & = & \mu_t + \epsilon_t \\ \epsilon_t & = & \sigma_t e_t \end{eqnarray}

    where \(\mu_t\) is the mean, \(\sigma^2_t\) is the conditional variance of \(\epsilon_t\) and \(e_t\) is the standardized shock, which has mean 0 and variance 1. An ARCH-type model specifies each of the three components: a mean model, a volatility process and a distribution for \(e_t\). Model parameters are estimated by maximum likelihood. The default estimator of the parameter covariance, cov_type="robust", corresponds in the ARCH literature to the estimator of Bollerslev and Wooldridge [BollerslevWooldridge1992]. The introduction shows how to build and estimate a model, and Forecasting and Model Results describe what can be done with it.

    Mean Models¶

    The mean model determines \(\mu_t\), see Mean Models.

    • ZeroMean, \(r_t = \epsilon_t\), and ConstantMean, \(r_t = \mu + \epsilon_t\).

    • LS, \(r_t = \mu + \gamma' x_t + \epsilon_t\), where \(x_t\) are exogenous regressors.

    • ARX and HARX, which are an autoregression and a heterogeneous autoregression, both with optional exogenous regressors.

    • MAMean, a moving average, \(r_t = \mu + \epsilon_t + \theta_1 \epsilon_{t-1} + \ldots + \theta_q \epsilon_{t-q}\).

    • ARCHInMean, in which a function \(f\) of the conditional variance enters the mean [EngleLilienRobins1987], \(r_t = \mu + \kappa f(\sigma^2_t) + \sum_{i=1}^p \phi_{L_{i}} r_{t-L_{i}} + \gamma' x_t + \epsilon_t\).

    Volatility Processes¶

    The volatility process determines \(\sigma^2_t\), see Volatility Processes. The GARCH class covers the ARCH, GARCH, GJR-GARCH, AVARCH, AVGARCH and TARCH specifications. Its variance dynamics, using the power \(\lambda\), are

    \[\sigma_{t}^{\lambda}=\omega + \sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}\left|\epsilon_{t-i}\right|^{\lambda} +\sum_{j=1}^{o}\gamma_{j}\left|\epsilon_{t-j}\right|^{\lambda} I\left[\epsilon_{t-j}<0\right]+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\beta_{k}\sigma_{t-k}^{\lambda}\]

    The GARCH(1,1) of the introduction is the case \(p=q=1\), \(o=0\) and \(\lambda=2\).

    Process

    Description

    ARCH

    \(\sigma_t^{2}=\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}\epsilon_{t-i}^{2}\) [Engle1982]

    GARCH

    ARCH, GARCH [Bollerslev1986], GJR-GARCH [GJR1993], AVARCH, AVGARCH and TARCH [Zakoian1994] specifications, using the equation above

    EGARCH

    \(\ln\sigma_{t}^{2}\) is a function of the lagged absolute and signed standardized shocks \(e_{t-i}=\epsilon_{t-i}/\sigma_{t-i}\) and of lagged \(\ln\sigma_{t}^{2}\) [Nelson1991]

    APARCH

    Asymmetric power ARCH, which models \(\sigma_t^{\delta}\) using the terms \((|\epsilon_{t-i}|-\gamma_i\epsilon_{t-i})^{\delta}\) [DingGrangerEngle1993]

    FIGARCH

    Fractionally integrated GARCH, which has a fractional differencing parameter \(d\) and is estimated using its truncated ARCH(\(\infty\)) representation [BaillieBollerslevMikkelsen1996]

    FIAPARCH

    Fractionally integrated asymmetric power ARCH, which extends the FIGARCH with the asymmetry and the power of the APARCH [Tse1998]

    HARCH

    Heterogeneous ARCH, in which the variance depends on averages of past squared shocks over several lag lengths

    MIDASHyperbolic

    Variance that depends on \(m\) lagged squared shocks, with weights \(\phi_i(\theta)\propto\Gamma(i+\theta)/(\Gamma(i+1)\Gamma(\theta))\) that sum to 1 [Foroni2013] [Sheppard2018]

    EWMAVariance

    \(\sigma_t^{2}=\lambda\sigma_{t-1}^2 + (1-\lambda)\epsilon^2_{t-1}\), with \(\lambda=0.94\) by default [RiskMetrics1996]

    RiskMetrics2006

    A weighted average of kmax EWMA variance processes

    ConstantVariance

    The same variance in all periods

    FixedVariance

    A variance that is provided by the user

    Distributions¶

    The distribution is the distribution of \(e_t\), which is used to construct the likelihood, see Distributions. All of the distributions are standardized to have mean 0 and variance 1.

    • Normal.

    • StudentsT, with one shape parameter \(\nu\) [Bollerslev1987].

    • SkewStudent, a skewed Student’s t with the tail shape \(\eta\) and the skewness \(\lambda\) [Hansen1994]. When \(\lambda=0\) it is the standardized Student’s t.

    • GeneralizedError, the generalized error distribution, with one shape parameter \(\nu\) [Nelson1991].
